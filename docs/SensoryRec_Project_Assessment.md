# SensoryRec — Capstone Project Assessment
**Reviewer role:** Acting as BDBA capstone supervisor + external thesis evaluator
**Date of review:** June 14, 2026
**Current academic milestone status:** Pre-proposal (Full Proposal due Oct 15, 2026)

---

## 1. Executive Summary

SensoryRec proposes a natural-language-driven hybrid recommender system for the LVMH Moët Hennessy wine portfolio, targeting the well-documented decline in wine engagement among 18–30-year-olds. The differentiating idea — letting users describe preferences in plain language ("fruity but not that fruity") rather than requiring wine vocabulary — is well grounded in the consumer-behaviour literature already gathered (label fluency, self-confidence, Gen Z discovery behaviour) and is reinforced by strong 2026 industry survey data showing that "improving my ability to find wines I enjoy" is the single largest driver of nonconsumer interest in wine.

Technically, the proposed architecture (content-based cold start → SBERT-based NLP taste embeddings → collaborative filtering on taste vectors → context-aware layer → explainability layer) maps almost one-to-one onto the recommender-systems course syllabus (S06, S12, S15, S18, S19, S21, S22), which is a major asset: every architectural choice can be grounded in taught material and cited course references.

At this stage, however, the project is closer to a **well-organized set of planning documents** than a research project. There are no formal research questions or hypotheses, the literature review is a pile of sources rather than a synthesis, the core NLP "vocabulary-agnostic" mechanism is described at the level of an aspiration rather than an algorithm, and the collaborative-filtering layer depends on synthetic/simulated user data whose validity is not yet addressed. The scope (5 layers + a spirits extension) is large for a single-author undergraduate capstone delivered over ~10 months while working full-time.

None of this is unusual for June, four months before the proposal deadline — but it means the next 8–10 weeks (before the semester locks in topic and scope) are the highest-leverage period in the entire project.

## 2. Assessment of Academic Quality

**Current state:** Pre-proposal scaffolding stage. The supporting documents (Data Strategy, Feature Engineering Strategy, EDA Report, Data Dictionary, Wine Enthusiast Dataset overview) are clear, well-organized, and demonstrate genuine understanding of the dataset — the EDA findings (43 countries, 707 varieties, price right-skewed at mean 35.36/median 25, missingness concentrated in `region_2` at 61%, `designation` at 29%, `taster_name` at 20%, `region_1` at 16%, `price` at 7%) are accurate and were verified against the sample data.

**Concerns:**

- **No research questions or hypotheses.** Everything in the brief is architectural/technical. A capstone needs at least one primary RQ (e.g., *"Can a natural-language preference elicitation layer improve perceived relevance of wine recommendations for novice consumers compared to attribute-based filtering, holding the underlying item representation constant?"*) and testable hypotheses tied to specific metrics.
- **Literature review is a corpus, not a review.** ~15 PDFs are present (CARS theory, collaborative filtering theory, content-based theory, cold-start/bias, explainability, bandits, SBERT, Gen Z consumer behaviour, label fluency, environmental attitudes, systematic CARS review). No synthesis document yet identifies themes, contradictions, or — critically — the **specific gap** SensoryRec fills (a vocabulary-agnostic NLP layer for wine recommendation aimed at novices is plausibly novel, but this claim needs to be demonstrated by the literature review, not asserted).
- **AI-policy exposure.** IE's stated policy is explicit: *"Do not submit any text directly generated by AI, without it being referenced like any external source... Violating this may result in an investigation for plagiarism."* Several existing project documents (Data Strategy, Feature Engineering Strategy, Wine Enthusiast Dataset overview, Data Dictionary descriptions) read as AI-generated scaffolding — templated bullet structures, generic phrasing, no citations. This is **fine as Paul's personal planning material**, but none of it can go into the written report as-is. Every section of the eventual thesis needs to be rewritten in Paul's own analytical voice, with any AI assistance disclosed per the policy. This is a real risk if not addressed early — a plagiarism finding caps the first-call grade at best and can fail it outright.
- **`[TO COMPLETE]` placeholders** reportedly remain in the capstone brief — these typically correspond to exactly the elements (RQs, hypotheses, evaluation ground truth) flagged above.

**Academic quality verdict:** Strong foundations, but the project currently lacks the formal research apparatus (RQs, hypotheses, synthesized literature, conceptual framework) that distinguishes a capstone from a product-development plan. This is fixable, and the timeline (proposal not due until Oct 15) allows for it — but it needs deliberate effort now, not organically during literature-review season.

## 3. Assessment of Technical Complexity

**This is a technically ambitious project** — arguably ambitious enough for two capstones. Breaking down the five proposed layers:

| Layer | Complexity | Course alignment | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Content-based cold-start recommender | Medium | S15, S16 | Conceptually solid; dataset well-suited |
| 2. NLP taste profiling (SBERT) | Medium-High | S02, S16 (SBERT paper present) | Embedding pipeline is standard; the **mapping from casual user language → embedding-space adjustment** is the genuinely novel/hard part and is currently unspecified |
| 3. Collaborative filtering on taste vectors | High (given constraints) | S12, S13 | **No real interaction data exists.** Synthetic user profiles must be generated and validated — this is non-trivial and affects every downstream evaluation metric |
| 4. Context-aware layer | High | S18, S19 | Theoretically well-supported (pre-filter/post-filter/contextual modeling all covered) but is typically itself a full module of work |
| 5. Explainable recommendations | Medium-High | S21, plus dedicated XAI papers (1804.11192, 2305.11755) | Achievable at a basic level (template-based, feature-attribution explanations); full XAI methods from the literature would be a stretch |

**Strengths:**
- The EDA is already done and correctly identifies that this dataset is strong for content-based/NLP work but weak for collaborative filtering — this honesty needs to carry through into the methodology rather than being quietly dropped.
- Feature engineering plan (SBERT `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` → 384-dim vectors; log-price; min-max scaled points; one-hot variety/country; target/one-hot region; composite "wine vector") is sound and standard practice.
- Sample data confirms the missingness patterns reported (e.g., in the 500-row sample: `region_2` missing 312/500, `designation` missing 131/500, `price` missing 29/500), so the imputation/exclusion strategy will need to be concrete, not aspirational.

**Concerns:**
- The **core innovation — natural-language, vocabulary-agnostic feedback ("fruity but not that fruity")** — is the part of the project with the *least* technical specification and the *most* academic novelty. This needs to become the centerpiece of the methodology, not a footnote. Candidate concrete approaches: (a) a small set of hand-labeled "casual phrase → sensory attribute adjustment" pairs used to fine-tune or calibrate a lightweight reranker on top of SBERT similarity; (b) prompt-based extraction of structured sensory deltas from free text, validated against the sommelier's judgments; (c) a contrastive learning setup using paired (casual description, professional tasting note) examples. Right now none of these is chosen.
- **Five layers + a spirits extension** is too much surface area for one person in ~10 months alongside a full-time job. Realistically, 2–3 layers can be built, evaluated, and written up rigorously; the rest will end up underbaked, which external readers notice immediately.
- Evaluation metrics listed (Precision@K, Recall@K, NDCG@K, Coverage, Diversity, Serendipity) all **require a notion of "relevant" items** — without real interaction data, this has to come from somewhere (synthetic ground truth, sommelier-labeled test cases, or a small user study). This pipeline isn't specified yet.

**Technical complexity verdict:** Genuinely strong, course-aligned, and ambitious — but currently over-scoped relative to what one person can execute with rigor in the available time, and under-specified exactly where the novelty lives.

## 4. Assessment of Business Relevance

This is the strongest part of the project as it stands.

- The L.E.K. Consulting 2026 wine consumer survey data already gathered is excellent supporting evidence: improving the ability to find wines one enjoys is the top-ranked reason nonconsumers say they would start drinking wine, ahead of more social occasions, lower prices, or greater familiarity. This is close to a direct empirical justification for SensoryRec's core premise.
- The Gen Z cohort entering legal drinking age is projected to grow roughly 80% over the next five years, and the premium segment (bottles ≥$15 retail) has shown relative resilience even as overall category demand has declined — both points strengthen the case for an LVMH-relevant, premium-tier recommendation tool.
- The LVMH Moët Hennessy framing, sommelier validator, and "where-to-buy via Wine-Searcher + geolocation" distribution layer all give the project a believable path from academic prototype to commercial pitch — which aligns directly with IE's stated learning objective of *"address[ing] real-world challenges and impact[ing] different collectives, industries, and ecosystems."*

**Concern:** the business case is currently stronger than the technical specification of *how* the product delivers on that case. The "vocabulary-agnostic" promise is the commercial hook, and right now it's also the least-defined technical component (see Section 3). Tightening this is simultaneously an academic and business priority.

## 5. Main Strengths

- A real, well-evidenced business problem with strong 2026 industry data directly supporting the project's central premise.
- A large, clean, well-understood public dataset (Wine Enthusiast, 129,971 reviews) with EDA already completed and correctly interpreted.
- An architecture whose every layer corresponds to a taught course module, making it straightforward to ground methodology choices in cited course material and the broader literature (Adomavicius & Tuzhilin on CARS, Reimers & Gurevych on SBERT, the systematic CARS review, the explainability surveys).
- A domain-expert validator (sommelier) available for qualitative grounding of recommendations — a genuine differentiator most peer capstones won't have.
- An explicit reproducibility commitment (GitHub version control of preprocessing, features, and models).
- A coherent "from problem to product" narrative connecting consumer behaviour literature → dataset → model → demo app → business application.

## 6. Main Weaknesses

- **No research questions or hypotheses** — the brief currently reads as a product spec, not a research proposal.
- **The literature review exists as a folder of PDFs**, not as a synthesized argument identifying themes, gaps, and SensoryRec's positioning.
- **The core NLP "casual-language-to-recommendation" mechanism is unspecified** — this is precisely the part reviewers will probe hardest, since it's the stated novelty.
- **Collaborative filtering relies on synthetic user profiles**, and there is currently no plan for how these will be generated, validated, or how this limitation will be honestly framed in the evaluation chapter.
- **Scope (5 layers + spirits extension) is large** relative to a single-author, part-time-study capstone.
- **AI-generated drafting** of multiple supporting documents creates exposure under IE's AI policy if not rewritten and properly cited before submission.
- **Evaluation framework is a metrics wishlist**, not a pipeline — Precision@K/Recall@K/NDCG@K all need a defined "ground truth" source that doesn't yet exist.
- **User study (10–20 participants)** has no recruitment, consent, or ethics plan yet.

## 7. Potential Risks

1. **Scope creep risk (highest):** if all five layers plus a spirits extension are pursued in parallel, none will reach the depth needed for a distinction — examiners consistently penalize "mile-wide, inch-deep" capstones more than narrow-but-deep ones.
2. **Validity risk on collaborative filtering:** synthetic interaction data, if presented uncritically, will likely be challenged by the external reader as not meeting the "address real-world challenges" rubric criterion. If framed honestly as a limitation with a mitigation (sommelier-validated synthetic profiles, or a small real user study used as ground truth for a subset), it becomes a strength instead.
3. **Plagiarism/AI-policy risk:** the existing AI-drafted documents must not be lightly edited and submitted — IE explicitly treats this as an investigable offense.
4. **Single-point-of-failure on the sommelier:** if this person's availability changes, a chunk of the qualitative validation plan disappears. Worth having a backup validation plan (e.g., a small panel of self-identified wine novices + one expert).
5. **Timeline risk:** the TFG schedule requires literature review (Jan 25, 2027), methodology (Feb 20, 2027), and full draft (Mar 20, 2027) in rapid succession during what is likely also exam season — front-loading work now (June–September 2026, before the semester even starts) is the main risk mitigation available.
6. **Supervisor-meeting requirement:** five meetings are mandatory (one before each deliverable); missing this tracked requirement can drop the grade by 10% independent of content quality.

Mapping SensoryRec against IE's 20 listed BDBA capstone topics:

## 10. Overall Project Grade Estimation (If Executed Successfully)

Two scenarios:

**A. Executed as currently scoped** (five layers attempted, synthetic CF presented without strong validation framing, NLP mechanism left at a "we used SBERT similarity" level, literature review remains list-like): a realistic outcome is the **6.5–7.5/10** range — competent ("Notable" territory) but unlikely to reach distinction, because examiners will identify exactly the gaps in Sections 6–8 above.

**B. Executed with the recommended adjustments** (1–2 RQs with clear hypotheses; synthesized literature review that positions SensoryRec against an identified gap; a concretely specified and evaluated NLP preference-mapping mechanism as the centerpiece; collaborative filtering reframed honestly as "proof-of-concept with synthetic/sommelier-validated profiles, with a roadmap to real data"; 2–3 layers built and rigorously evaluated rather than 5 attempted shallowly; a working demo; reproducible GitHub repo): this project is **realistically capable of 8.5–9.5/10** ("Sobresaliente"/distinction territory). The combination of strong business framing, a genuinely defensible NLP novelty claim, course-aligned methodology, and a domain-expert validator gives it more upside than most peer projects — *if* the academic apparatus (RQs, lit synthesis, evaluation rigor) catches up to the technical and business framing.

**What would most likely block a distinction grade, specifically:**
1. Submitting collaborative-filtering results based on synthetic data as if they were validated findings, without prominent, honest limitation framing.
2. Leaving the "natural language → recommendation" mechanism vague — this is the project's central claim and needs to be the most rigorously specified and evaluated part, not the least.
3. Spreading effort across 5 layers + spirits extension instead of going deep on 2–3.
4. Any written-report text that reads as unedited AI output without disclosure, under IE's explicit AI policy.
5. Missing the 5 mandatory supervisor meetings (automatic 10% deduction regardless of content).
